# Third-party software and data

DynaMol's MIT license covers its original application code. It does not replace the licenses of bundled fonts or installed scientific/JavaScript dependencies. Full license texts are included in the respective installed packages and upstream repositories. The self-contained macOS app collects dependency notices and an inventory under `DynaMol.app/Contents/Resources/Notices`. A separate source-materials companion records selected upstream archives, build recipes, patches and provenance. These records do not claim legal certification or a bit-identical rebuild.

- [NGL](https://github.com/nglviewer/ngl) supplies the molecular renderer and depends on Three.js.
- [React](https://github.com/facebook/react), [Vite](https://github.com/vitejs/vite), [Lucide](https://github.com/lucide-icons/lucide), [TypeScript](https://github.com/microsoft/TypeScript), and [Playwright](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright) supply interface/build/test components.
- [FastAPI](https://github.com/fastapi/fastapi), [Uvicorn](https://github.com/encode/uvicorn), [NumPy](https://github.com/numpy/numpy), and [MDTraj](https://github.com/mdtraj/mdtraj) supply the local API and scientific processing. MDTraj is LGPL software; its license and source remain available separately.
- [OpenMM](https://github.com/openmm/openmm) and [GROMACS](https://gitlab.com/gromacs/gromacs) are independently maintained MD engines, with their own licenses, force-field attribution, scientific references, and distribution requirements. The macOS app bundles OpenMM and GROMACS 2025.4. Native GROMACS binaries are not committed to this source repository; source-checkout installations discover their engine separately.
- [PDBFixer](https://github.com/openmm/pdbfixer) supplies missing-atom and sequence-supported residue reconstruction; [RDKit](https://github.com/rdkit/rdkit) supplies small-molecule parsing and conformer generation. Their upstream licenses and scientific references apply independently. Optional structure fetch uses [RCSB PDB](https://www.rcsb.org/) and [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/).
- [ProMod3](https://openstructure.org/promod3/) supplies local fragment-based loop modeling under Apache-2.0, using its separately bundled OpenStructure/OpenMM environment and data. Only requested missing coordinates are transferred; the method and scientific citation are described in [loop repair](docs/LOOP_REPAIR.md). Dependency notices and source-material records apply to this runtime separately from the MD engine.
- [AmberTools](https://ambermd.org/AmberTools.php), pinned to 24.8 by the private source installer and bundled in the macOS app, supplies Antechamber/SQM AM1-BCC, GAFF2 typing, Parmchk2 and LEaP. Its installed `LICENSE` and `licenses/` describe GPLv3 defaults, component exceptions and third-party licenses; this is not a blanket license for all Amber products. Force-field files under `dat/leap` are public-domain data according to that distribution's license. Scientific attribution remains appropriate for AM1-BCC, GAFF/GAFF2 and protein/water models. The native environment is not committed to this source repository. The companion includes the recipe-pinned base archive, Conda recipe patches and all eight numbered upstream updates used by the shipped 24.8 recipe. Original update bytes and their mapping are retained; the collector has not executed a full rebuild.
- [ParmEd](https://github.com/ParmEd/ParmEd) converts native Amber topologies to OpenMM XML; its license and subcomponent notices apply. [Gemmi](https://github.com/project-gemmi/gemmi) reads original mmCIF chemistry and identifiers, with MPL-2.0/LGPL-3.0 licensing options documented upstream. [Dimorphite-DL](https://github.com/durrantlab/dimorphite_dl) supplies heuristic ionization proposals under Apache-2.0. These utilities do not establish the correctness of a binding-site protonation state.
- The [wwPDB Chemical Component Dictionary](https://www.wwpdb.org/data/ccd) supplies reference chemical identities, atom names, bond orders and stereochemical annotations under the [wwPDB archive usage policy](https://www.wwpdb.org/about/usage-policies). DynaMol records provenance and caches reference files; a canonical display PDB can abbreviate a component name and is not the authoritative dictionary identity.
- Curated modified-residue force-field XML is vendored from [OpenMM forcefields at commit `3f73b6ff9730e4e356fc6a0dbde6039517d59412`](https://github.com/openmm/openmmforcefields/tree/3f73b6ff9730e4e356fc6a0dbde6039517d59412), under that repository's [MIT license](https://github.com/openmm/openmmforcefields/blob/3f73b6ff9730e4e356fc6a0dbde6039517d59412/LICENSE). [The local data directory](backend/data/modified_residues/) contains raw `phosaa14SB`/`ff14SB` XML, DynaMol's normalized file, source hashes, and the complete [upstream license notice](backend/data/modified_residues/OPENMMFORCEFIELDS_LICENSE.txt). The phosphorylated-residue parameters cite Raguette et al., *phosaa14SB and phosaa19SB: Updated Amber Force Field Parameters for Phosphorylated Amino Acids*, JCTC 20, 7199–7209 (2024). Normalization and the native PTR improper-order correction are described in [Modified amino-acid preparation](docs/MODIFIED_RESIDUES.md); the original upstream data and DynaMol's changes remain distinguishable.
- The same curated directory includes SEP, TPO, PTR and HYP Chemical Component Dictionary records retrieved from RCSB. These supply reference identities and ideal geometry for missing-atom templates. PDB archive data are provided under [CC0 1.0, as stated by wwPDB](https://www.wwpdb.org/about/usage-policies); source URLs and hashes accompany the records. Ideal coordinates are modeled templates, not independently validated reconstructions of a user's structure.
- The packaged Python interpreter is supplied by [python-build-standalone](https://github.com/astral-sh/python-build-standalone); its Python distribution metadata and license materials accompany the app. [conda-pack](https://conda.github.io/conda-pack/) is used to build and relocate the included native runtime archives; its upstream license and the independently licensed packages inside each archive apply separately.
- [DM Sans](https://github.com/googlefonts/dm-fonts) and [Manrope](https://github.com/sharanda/manrope), served locally through Fontsource, are licensed under the SIL Open Font License.
- Experimental coordinates for the bundled example come from [wwPDB / RCSB PDB entry 1UBQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1UBQ). PDB archive data are made freely available under [wwPDB's data policy](https://www.wwpdb.org/about/usage-policies). The example's simulated coordinates were generated with OpenMM and are labeled separately from the experimental input.

Dependency versions are recorded by `uv.lock`, `frontend/package-lock.json`, native environment exports and job/ligand provenance. Ligand archives also record native runtime details, parameter-data hashes and commands. The built app records its actual binary/Python inventory in `Notices/DEPENDENCY_INVENTORY.json` and source/archive hashes in `Contents/Resources/manifest.json`.

## Source-materials companion

The separate [companion guide](packaging/source-materials/README.md) describes the supplied collection. `INDEX.json` maps exact package versions/builds and binary-package hashes to the files under `archives/` and `materials/`. Native materials preserve shipped recipes, resolved build configurations, patches and notices; Python materials preserve distribution metadata and compiled-extension hashes. Keep the index, archives, checksums and matching release manifest together.

The selected collection includes GROMACS, AmberTools, LGPL native libraries, ParmEd, MDTraj, Gemmi, certifi and OpenMM's exact reported source revision. OpenMM's core/CPU/application layers are MIT-licensed; its OpenCL platform plugins are LGPL. It is not labeled wholly copyleft. Packages with a recorded non-GPL alternative are identified separately. GCC runtime sources are included conservatively with the runtime exception distinguished; inclusion alone is not a conclusion that every package requires a source archive.

AmberTools materials include the recipe's `AmberTools24_rc5.tar.bz2` base and official `update.1` through `update.8`, plus Conda patches and build metadata. The original `update.5` contains an `update.1` heading; its filename, upstream URL and checksum identify the preserved bytes. Full patch application and compilation were not rerun by the collector. AmberTools inclusion grants no rights to proprietary Amber/pmemd.

The permissively licensed SciPy wheel contains compiler-runtime libraries whose binary hashes, license/exception texts and build configuration are recorded. The companion also supplies official GCC 13.4.0 sources, checked against the upstream release checksum, as a conservative supplement matching its recorded compiler version. That match alone does not establish the precise source provenance of the vendored libgfortran, libquadmath or libgcc binaries. That provenance limitation and exception applicability remain explicit conditional records. Original compilers, SDKs and build dependencies are separate; neither the collection nor its verification asserts complete reproducibility, every transitive license interpretation, or legal certification.

## Installation evidence

The unsigned Apple Silicon app includes real engines and runs without separate development tools or MD installations. Exact-package acceptance and an additional test blocking reads from developer installations passed on the development Mac. The latter used an external polling adjustment for macOS's sandbox restriction on setuid `ps`; the app files and filesystem restrictions were unchanged. The matching release-side acceptance files identify the tested payload and final DMG's parity/copy checks. An independent clean-Mac check is not claimed. See [packaging evidence and limits](docs/PACKAGING.md).
